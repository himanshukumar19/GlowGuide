Title: Niacinamide - Mechanisms of Action and Its Topical Use in Dermatology
Source: PubMed
URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24993939/
Authors: Wohlrab J, Kreft D
Year: 2014

---

Niacinamide, the amide form of vitamin B3, is a water-soluble compound whose 
skin effects have been studied extensively. When applied topically at sufficient 
concentrations, niacinamide shows antipruritic (anti-itch), antimicrobial, 
vasoactive, photo-protective, sebum-regulating, and skin-brightening effects, 
with the degree of each effect depending on concentration used. Mechanistically, 
niacinamide influences NFκB-mediated signaling pathways by inhibiting an enzyme 
called PARP-1, which plays a role in regulating inflammatory and stress responses 
in skin cells. This mechanistic basis supports its widespread use as an active 
ingredient in dermatological and cosmetic formulations targeting inflammation, 
oil production, and pigmentation concerns.
