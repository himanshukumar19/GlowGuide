Title: Niacinamide - Ingredient Overview
Source: INCIDecoder

Retrieved: 2026-07-14

---

Niacinamide (also known as vitamin B3 or nicotinamide) is a water-soluble vitamin, 
typically found as an odorless white powder with a mildly salty, bitter taste. It 
occurs naturally in foods like meat, fish, and wheat, though the version used in 
skincare products is produced synthetically.

Key benefits:
- Widely used as an anti-aging and wrinkle-smoothing ingredient, typically at 
  4-5% concentration
- Helps fade hyperpigmentation and dark spots, with stronger results when 
  combined with acetyl glucosamine; studies show 2-5% niacinamide used daily 
  for 8 weeks produces significant lightening of dark spots
- Works differently from most other skin-lightening ingredients — rather than 
  blocking the tyrosinase enzyme (as vitamin C, arbutin, and kojic acid do), 
  niacinamide suppresses the transfer of pigment-carrying melanosomes from 
  melanocytes to keratinocytes, making it effective to combine with tyrosinase 
  inhibitors for a multi-pronged approach to pigmentation
- Increases ceramide synthesis, strengthening the skin barrier and improving 
  hydration
- Has sebum-regulating and anti-inflammatory properties; a study using 4% 
  niacinamide over 8 weeks for acne found 82% of participants showed improvement, 
  performing slightly better than a 1% clindamycin (antibiotic) gel
- May help with rosacea and atopic dermatitis due to its anti-inflammatory effects
- Also supports epidermal cell turnover, wound healing, skin texture, antibacterial 
  activity, and photoprotection
