Title: The Mechanism of Retinol-Induced Irritation and Its Application to Anti-Irritant Development
Source: PubMed
URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14615068/
Authors: Kim et al.
Year: 2003

---

Despite its well-documented dermatological benefits, retinol and its derivatives 
commonly cause local skin irritation, presenting as mild redness (erythema) and 
peeling of the outer skin layer (stratum corneum). Researchers hypothesized that 
cytokines act as key inflammatory mediators in this retinoid-induced irritation. 
Using mouse epidermal cells treated with 2% retinol, the study measured changes 
in inflammatory cytokine expression, including MCP-1 and IL-8, via RT-PCR 
analysis. Results confirmed that retinoid-induced inflammation is primarily 
mediated through increased MCP-1 and IL-8 levels, both at the mRNA and protein 
level, in human epidermal keratinocytes, melanocytes, and fibroblasts. The study 
also screened several natural compounds (including beta-sitosterol, Ginkgo 
extract, and raspberry extract) as potential anti-irritant additives to 
counteract this inflammatory response.
