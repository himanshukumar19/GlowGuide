Title: How to Fade Dark Spots in Darker Skin Tones
Source: American Academy of Dermatology (AAD)
URL: https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-secrets/routine/fade-dark-spots
Retrieved: 2026-07-14

---

Dark spots and patches are among the most common reasons people with darker skin 
tones seek dermatological care. One common cause is post-inflammatory 
hyperpigmentation (PIH), often shortened to just "hyperpigmentation" — this occurs 
when something triggers the skin to overproduce melanin, the pigment responsible 
for skin color.

Common triggers of hyperpigmentation in medium-to-dark skin tones include:
- Skin conditions such as acne, eczema, or psoriasis — the underlying condition 
  must be treated first before the resulting dark spots can fade
- Skin injury — as a wound heals, a dark spot or patch may appear, which typically 
  fades over time if the skin isn't re-injured
- Irritating skincare or haircare products — switching to gentler products can 
  help prevent new dark spots from forming

Treatment approach:
- Finding and addressing the root cause is the first step — many dark spots fade 
  naturally once the underlying trigger is removed
- Dermatologist-recommended treatments may include laser therapy or 
  prescription-strength hydroquinone cream; one case example noted improvement 
  within about 8 weeks using this combination
- If a particular treatment doesn't produce satisfactory results, consulting a 
  dermatologist is recommended for further evaluation

Prevention (since hyperpigmentation can recur):
- Apply sunscreen every two hours, and reapply after swimming or sweating
- Wear a wide-brimmed hat when outdoors
- Try to limit sun exposure during peak intensity hours
